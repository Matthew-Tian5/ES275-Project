Species Profile

Meet the Red-headed Woodpecker

Melanerpes erythrocephalus

Red-headed Woodpecker

An endangered species is a species that lives in the wild in Ontario but is facing imminent extinction or extirpation.

The Red-headed Woodpecker was once more common throughout southern Ontario. It was already recognized as being at risk when Ontario's Endangered Species Act took effect in 2008, highlighting longstanding concerns about its survival.

Look out for

A medium-sized bird, about 20 centimetres long, with a vivid red head, neck, and breast. Its wings and back are mostly black, while its underside is mostly white.

Preferred habitat

Red-headed Woodpeckers live in open woodlands and woodland edges, including parks, golf courses, and cemeteries. They rely on dead trees for nesting and perching.

They often winter in the United States where acorns and beechnuts are available, although a few remain in southern Ontario when suitable nut supplies remain.

Where to find them

They are widespread but rare across southern Ontario. They may also be found in Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Quebec, and are relatively common in the United States.

Threats

  • Loss of nesting sites and habitat from deforestation and development
  • Reduction in food supply
  • Collisions with buildings, vehicles, towers, and power lines
  • Competition with introduced European Starlings for nesting sites
  • Predators including cats, Cooper's Hawks, and Sharp-shinned Hawks

Limitations

  • Dependence on open woodland and dead or dying trees
  • Scarce nesting cavities, especially where dead trees are removed
  • Dependence on seasonal insects, acorns, and beechnuts
  • Small, rare populations that are slow to recover
  • Winter survival that depends on habitats outside Ontario

Quick facts

  • Feathers covering the nostrils keep out wood particles while digging.
  • A long, barbed tongue helps reach insects in cracks and crevices.
  • Saliva coats the tongue with a glue-like substance that helps capture insects.
  • They hide insects and seeds in wood cracks, under bark, fence posts, and roof shingles.

How you can help

  • Volunteer with local nature clubs or provincial parks for surveys and stewardship work.
  • Private landowners can protect habitat and may qualify for species recovery stewardship programs.
  • Report illegal activity to 1-866-MOE-TIPS (663-8477).

Conservation efforts

Bird Studies Canada supports the understanding, appreciation, and conservation of wild birds and their habitats. Learn more at Birds Canada.

Carolinian Canada helps recover species at risk and protect habitats across southern Ontario. Learn more at Carolinian Canada.
